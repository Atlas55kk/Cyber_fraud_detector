================================================================================
SIH PROBLEM STATEMENT: PS 26183 — Crypto Wallet Fraud Tracing (MHA)
================================================================================

ORGANIZATION / MINISTRY: 
Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA), Government of India / Cybercrime Investigation

SUMMARY:
"When someone reports a scam wallet, follow the stolen money and find where it gets cashed out."

WHAT YOU'RE ACTUALLY BUILDING:
A tool that takes a crypto wallet address (reported by a scam victim), looks up its 
transaction history, follows the money as it hops through other wallets, and flags 
the point where it likely reaches a real exchange (where it could be frozen).

ABSOLUTE BASICS:
- Blockchain: Public ledger of every transaction ever made, viewable by anyone, forever.
- Wallet Address: Account identifier (e.g., 0x7a3b...).
- Transaction: Money moving from one address to another (with amount, timestamp, gas/fee, token/ETH).

CORE SYLLABUS / BUILDING BLOCKS:
1. Reading blockchain data via APIs (Etherscan, Blockchair, Blockchain.info, RPC nodes).
2. Graph theory basics: Wallets as "nodes", transactions as directed "edges", graph traversal (BFS/DFS).
3. Address clustering heuristics: Techniques to identify addresses belonging to the same entity/scammer.
4. Risk-scoring logic: Hop distance to known exchanges, velocity of money, split ratios, time decay.
5. Known exchange address attribution: Matching destination addresses against verified exchange clusters (Binance, WazirX, Coinbase, etc.).
6. Graph visualization: Visualizing the money trail (vis.js, D3.js, Cytoscape, Neo4j Bloom).
7. Plain-language investigator summary: Forensic summary for LEAs (Law Enforcement Agencies) highlighting actionable off-ramps/exchanges to issue freeze notices.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE REAL-WORLD INVESTIGATION FLOW & CHALLENGE:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Victim reports a scam transaction (e.g., sent Rs 50,000 / crypto to Wallet S).
2. Police only have: Scammer's address (Wallet S).
3. Scammer splits and hops funds:
   Wallet S -> Wallet B (Rs 20k) & Wallet C (Rs 30k)
   Wallet B -> Wallet D -> Wallet G (Binance deposit address -> actionable!)
   Wallet C -> Wallet E & F -> Wallet H & I (open branches, peeling chains, mixing).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE RESEARCH CORE (OUR TARGET RESEARCH PROBLEM):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Notice in my example, the tree only had 6-7 wallets and I could draw the whole thing by hand.
In reality, scammers often deliberately split funds across hundreds or thousands of wallets 
specifically to make tracing exhausting (mixing, peel chains, chain hopping, smurfing).

At that scale:
1. Combinatorial Explosion / Path Pruning: You cannot print every path — there are too many.
2. Probabilistic Flow Attribution / Taint Analysis: Decide which paths are 'worth following' 
   vs background noise (e.g., calculating probability/taint proportion of stolen funds vs 
   unrelated concurrent volume at intermediate nodes).
3. Latency & Performance: Must compute and traverse efficiently even across graphs of tens 
   of thousands of nodes and edges."

This research frontier is the differentiator that elevates this project from a trivial API viewer 
into an algorithmic, defensible forensic intelligence engine.
